# Informational Interview Guide

A reusable guide for natural networking conversations: learning what a role and team are really like, not asking for a job.

**Public vs private:** this file is a template. Prep and notes about a specific person go in `private/networking/` (git-ignored), using `private/networking/TEMPLATE_person.md`.

## The steps

1. **Pick who to talk to.** Someone doing the job you want (a TAM or CSM), or someone who manages that team. Check `private/contacts.md` and the `Ask:` notes on your leads in `job_leads.md`.
2. **Prepare (15 minutes).** Copy the person template into `private/networking/`. Write down why them, how you're connected, and 3 to 5 questions you most want answered. If they work at a company you might interview with, include 1 or 2 **briefcase questions** (below): their answers become the raw material for your briefcase proposal.
3. **Ask for the conversation.** Use a request template below. Keep it short, specific, and easy to say yes to.
4. **Have the conversation (15 to 20 minutes).** Be curious, listen more than you talk, and watch the time.
5. **Follow up within 24 hours.** Send a thank-you, and anything you promised.
6. **Write notes the same day** in their private file: what you learned, and any next steps. Copy anything useful for a briefcase into `private/briefcase/<company>.md`.

## Request templates

**Short message (LinkedIn or email):**

> Hi [first name], I'm exploring [TAM / customer success] roles at [type of company] and noticed you [specific detail: your role at X, your post about Y]. Would you be open to a 15-minute call in the next couple of weeks? I'd love to hear what the work is really like day to day. No worries at all if the timing doesn't work.

**Through a mutual connection:**

> Hi [first name], [mutual connection] suggested I reach out. They said you'd be a great person to ask about [topic]. I'm exploring [role] roles and would really value 15 minutes of your perspective. Would that be possible in the next couple of weeks?

## Question bank

Pick the 4 to 6 that matter most for this person. Leave room to follow their answers.

**The work itself**
- What does a typical week look like for you?
- What surprised you most when you started this role?
- What makes someone great at this job, compared with good?

**Customers and honesty**
- How do you balance what's best for the customer with company goals?
- Have you ever told a customer *not* to buy something? How was that received?

**Sales duties**
- Are there renewal or upsell targets in your role? What are they like day to day?
- Do you ever feel pressure to sell things customers don't need?

**Technical depth**
- How much scripting or coding do you do? Which tools or languages?
- What would you learn first if you were starting over?

**Team and working style**
- How does the team work across time zones? Are Pacific hours common?
- How much do you travel, and what for (customers, conferences, offsites)?

**Briefcase questions** (for people at companies you might interview with)

These help you understand the team's real problems, so a later briefcase proposal (see `interview_practice/briefcase_template.md`) is grounded in what you heard, not guesses. Write the answers in the person's private file, and treat anything sensitive as confidential.

- What are the biggest challenges your customers run into after they buy?
- Where do customers most often get stuck during onboarding or adoption?
- What's the team's biggest priority or problem this year?
- What would a new person in this role ideally accomplish in their first 90 days?
- What do you wish customers understood better about the product?
- If you had more time, what would you improve about how the team works?

**Closing**
- Is there anything I didn't ask that I should have?
- Is there anyone else you think I should talk to?

## During the conversation

- Start with a quick thank-you and a one-line reason you're reaching out.
- Let them talk. Short follow-ups such as "What was that like?" go a long way.
- Don't ask for a job or a referral. If they offer to help, say thank you.
- End on time, and ask whether there's anything you can do for them.

## Follow-up templates

**Thank-you (within 24 hours):**

> Hi [first name], thank you so much for your time today. I especially appreciated what you said about [specific thing]. It gave me a lot to think about. [If relevant: I'll take a look at (resource they mentioned).] I hope we can stay in touch.

**Later update (a few weeks on):**

> Hi [first name], I wanted to share a quick update: I followed up on [their suggestion], and [what happened]. Thanks again for your advice. It really helped.
