# Interview Practice Prompt

Copy the prompt below into Claude to practice a mock interview. Fill in the brackets first.

To use your own stories, ask Claude to read `private/interview_practice/my_stories.md`, or paste the relevant ones in. Save transcripts and feedback in `private/interview_practice/`, not in this folder.

```text
Let's do a mock interview. Act as the interviewer for this role:

- Role: [for example, Technical Account Manager]
- Company or type of company: [for example, a cybersecurity SaaS company]
- Stage: [recruiter screen / hiring manager / technical / panel]
- Job posting (optional): [paste the requirements]

How to run it:
1. Ask me one question at a time, and wait for my answer.
2. Mix behavioral, technical and customer-scenario questions that fit the role.
   Use interview_practice/question_bank.md as a starting point if it helps.
3. After each answer, give brief feedback:
   - one thing that worked
   - one thing to improve
   - whether I used STAR (situation, task, action, result) clearly
4. Ask a natural follow-up question sometimes, as a real interviewer would.
5. After [5] questions, stop and give me a summary:
   - my strongest answer and why
   - my 2 biggest areas to work on
   - one question I should practice again

Be encouraging but honest. If an answer is vague, tell me.
```

## Practicing your briefcase

To practice bringing up and presenting your briefcase, add this to the end of the prompt above, with your briefcase from `private/briefcase/` pasted in:

```text
I have a briefcase proposal (below) that I'll ask to share near the
beginning of the interview, for example: "Would it be possible for me to
share my screen at some point? I put together something I'd like to show
you."

1. Start the interview as usual, and give me a chance to make that request
   early on.
2. Respond the way a real interviewer might. Vary it between practice
   rounds: sometimes say "go ahead now", sometimes "let's save it for the
   end", and sometimes sound a little unsure, so I practice each case.
3. When I present it, react as a real hiring manager would: ask follow-up
   questions and push back on one idea.
4. In your feedback, tell me how my request came across (confident and
   natural, or awkward), what landed in the briefcase, what felt unclear,
   and whether it felt helpful rather than presumptuous.

[paste your briefcase here]
```
