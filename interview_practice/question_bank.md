# Interview Question Bank: TAM and CSM Roles

Common questions for technical account manager and customer success roles, grouped by theme. These are general practice questions, not from any specific company.

**Public vs private:** your answers and stories go in `private/interview_practice/my_stories.md` (git-ignored), never in this file.

## How to answer behavioral questions (STAR)

- **Situation:** the context, in 1 or 2 sentences.
- **Task:** what you were responsible for.
- **Action:** what *you* did. This is the most important part.
- **Result:** what happened, with a number if you have one, and what you learned.

Aim for about 1.5 to 2 minutes per answer.

## Where the briefcase fits

A briefcase is a short written proposal: the challenges you've learned the team or its customers face, and how you'd help in the role. Build it with `interview_practice/briefcase_template.md`, and keep each company's version in `private/briefcase/`.

- **When:** any interview, from a recruiter screen to a final round. Match the depth to the stage: a few short points for a recruiter, a fuller version for a hiring manager or panel.
- **Bring it up near the beginning.** Ask early so the interviewer can plan for it, then let them choose when. In a virtual interview, for example: *"Before we dive in, would it be possible for me to share my screen at some point? I put together something I'd like to show you."* In person, offer a printed copy instead.
- **Then follow their lead.** If they say "now", present it. If they say "later" or "at the end", keep it ready and look for a natural moment, such as "Do you have any questions for us?" or "How would you approach the first 90 days?"
- **How:** keep it to one or two pages, or a few slides, with screen sharing set up and the file open before the call. Present it as ideas to check with them ("Here's what I noticed; does this match what you see?"), not as answers.
- **Before salary talks:** it's most useful before compensation comes up, because it shows the value you'd bring.
- **Honesty:** only include what you actually learned or can source, and say which parts are your assumptions.

## Customer relationships

- Tell me about a time you turned around an unhappy customer.
- How do you build trust with a new customer who's skeptical of your product?
- Tell me about a time you had to tell a customer something they didn't want to hear.
- How do you manage many accounts at once? How do you decide where to spend your time?

## Technical problem solving

- Walk me through how you'd troubleshoot an issue a customer reports but you can't reproduce.
- Explain a technical concept you know well to someone non-technical.
- Tell me about a time you worked with engineering to fix a customer issue.
- How do you learn a new product or technology quickly?

## Adoption and value

- How do you measure whether a customer is getting value?
- Tell me about a time you increased a customer's adoption of a product.
- What early signs tell you a customer might not renew?

## Commercial (if the role has sales duties)

- How do you approach a renewal conversation?
- How do you spot when a customer needs more, without pushing?
- Tell me about a time you said no to a sale because it wasn't right for the customer.

## Working style

- Why customer success or technical account management?
- Why this company, and why this industry (cybersecurity, climate, healthtech, responsible AI)?
- Tell me about a time you disagreed with a colleague. How did you resolve it?
- How do you stay organized across time zones in a remote team?

## Questions to ask them

- How is this role measured? Is any of the pay tied to renewals or expansion?
- What does success look like in the first 90 days?
- How much of the role is technical, and which tools does the team use?
- How does the team support people working Pacific hours?
- What's something you'd change about the role if you could?
